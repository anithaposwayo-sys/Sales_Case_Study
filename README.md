 **Sales Case Study Analysis**
📌 Project Overview
This project analyses data to identify sales trends, product performance, peak trading periods, pricing patterns, and profitability.
The goal was to transform raw transactional data into meaningful business insights that could support sales performance monitoring, pricing decisions, product strategy, and promotional planning.

The project demonstrates an end-to-end data analytics workflow, from data cleaning and preparation to analysis, KPI development, dashboard creation, and business recommendations.

**🎯 Business Objectives**

The analysis was designed to answer the following business questions:

What is the daily sales price per unit?
What is the average unit sales price?
What is the daily gross profit percentage?
What is the daily gross profit percentage per unit?
During selected promotional periods:
What was the Price Elasticity of Demand (PED)?
How did sales volume change?
How did pricing affect demand?
Which product types generate the most revenue?
What are the busiest periods for sales?
Which products or categories have the strongest sales performance?

**📊 Key Metrics**
The project calculated several important commercial and sales metrics.

💰 Daily Sales Price per Unit

The daily sales price per unit was calculated to understand how the selling price changed over time.

📈 Average Unit Sales Price

The average unit sales price was calculated to provide an overall view of pricing during the period analysed.

💵 Gross Profit %

Gross profit percentage was calculated to measure profitability relative to sales.

Formula:

Gross Profit % = (Revenue - Cost) / Revenue × 100
💵 Gross Profit % per Unit

Gross profit per unit was analysed to understand the profitability generated by individual units sold.

📉 Price Elasticity of Demand

One of the key analytical components of the project was measuring Price Elasticity of Demand (PED) during selected promotional periods.

PED was used to examine the relationship between changes in price and changes in quantity demanded.

Formula:

PED = % Change in Quantity Demanded
      -------------------------------
          % Change in Price

Three promotional periods were selected for comparison.

The analysis examined whether changes in price were associated with:

Increased demand
Decreased demand
Relatively stable demand

This provided a practical example of how pricing decisions can influence sales volume.
